How the district map was built

No official district map matching the 2023 census is openly published, so this one is assembled from census building blocks. The 2017 census tehsils are each assigned to their 2023 census district by matching names, then merged into district shapes. Of 553 tehsils, 381 matched a census tehsil name exactly, 74 matched a close spelling inside the same district, 19 come from Gilgit-Baltistan's own district layer, and 79 were assigned by hand with a written reason. Each district carries a confidence label taken from its least certain tehsil.

The result is 156 districts: 36 in Punjab, 30 in Sindh, 35 in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, 34 in Balochistan, Islamabad, 10 in Azad Jammu and Kashmir and 10 in Gilgit-Baltistan.

Known limits

  • Karachi's seven districts are drawn from the city's older towns and cantonments. Where a town was split between two of today's districts, the whole town sits in one of them, so Karachi East and Karachi South are marked low confidence.
  • Azad Jammu and Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan are mapped by district but are not covered by Pakistan's Census 2023, so they show no census figures. Gilgit-Baltistan uses its 10 long-standing districts; districts announced since 2019 are not yet drawn separately.
  • Tando Muhammad Khan: one tehsil is missing from the census area table, so its area and density are left blank. Its urban share and sex ratio come from the age table instead. All its other figures are complete.
  • Household tables (housing, water, schooling, disability) count people living in households. People in institutions such as barracks and hostels are excluded, so these tables add up to about 0.4% less than the official population.
  • Balochistan's growth rates between 2017 and 2023 are very high in several districts (up to 9.5% a year). These are the published census figures; their accuracy has been publicly debated.
  • The area labelled "Disputed Territory" in the source tehsil layer lies outside every district. It is drawn as a separate shaded area and has no figures.
  • Areas come from the census tables, not from the map, because the map's lines are approximate at the edges.

Corrections

Zila Atlas is an early preview. Every figure links to its source and carries a confidence label, and some figures are still being checked against original filings.

Found a wrong number, a misplaced tehsil or a misspelled name? Report it here (a free GitHub account is needed), or tell the person who sent you the link. Every correction is logged with the date and what changed, and the affected tables are rebuilt. Corrections so far: none.
